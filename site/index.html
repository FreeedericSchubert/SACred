Enzyme sites as single-atom catalysts

Hydrogenases, laccases and nitrogenases hold single metal atoms in pockets of N, S and O donors. We took the first coordination shell of every transition-metal site in the Protein Data Bank, rebuilt it as a doped vacancy in graphene and computed how strongly each site binds hydrogen. The target is a hydrogen adsorption free energy ΔGH near 0 eV: binding neither too strongly nor too weakly.

Has anyone made these before?

For the sites near the optimum we searched PubMed, arXiv and chemRxiv (Valency), read the closest abstracts, then read the open-access full texts of the closest papers, followed their citations and searched for screening series such as M–NxO4−x. A site counts as reported if a paper has the same metal with exactly the same donor atoms on a carbon support, including large DFT screenings that compute it without naming it.

From the PDB to six sites

Most of the drop is merging, not rejecting. The PDB holds the same few coordination motifs thousands of times, and graphene keeps only the donor set and its arrangement, so many protein sites become one graphene site. The real filters come later: frequency, the fast xTB screen, compute time and the narrow window around ΔGH = 0.

    Fast screening first

    MoS2: basal plane and an Fe–S3 site

    On the basal-plane S of clean MoS2 hydrogen binds far too weakly (ΔGH = +2.09 eV). An Fe bound to three S of the sheet (like an Fe of an Fe–S cluster), puts H on the Fe at ΔGH = +0.11 eV, close to the optimum; the runs with the sheet charged against a plate below (doping from underneath) were not started. The xTB values for Fe and eleven other metals on the same site are in the next section; for Fe, xTB is about 1.2 eV too negative here.

    MoS2 and silica pick other enzyme sites

    On graphene the vacancy supplies the donors. On MoS2 the sheet supplies three S, 3.16 Å apart, to a metal sitting over a Mo; in a sulfur vacancy the metal binds the three Mo below instead. On silica, condensation with Si–OH supplies one to three siloxide O. Mapping every PDB site onto these faces selects other enzyme sites than graphene does: MoS2 fits the octahedral and trigonal-prismatic sulfur sites of hydrogenases and of W and Mo formate dehydrogenases, while the tetrahedral Fe–S cubes are about 0.5 Å too wide; silica takes oxygen-rich sites such as the water-bound zinc of carbonic anhydrase. Whatever the support does not supply would have to come from a thiol on MoS2 or a silane linker on silica (“on top”).

    Hydrogen on the bare sites, first look with GFN2-xTB

    GFN2-xTB, spin-restricted, relaxed. MoS2: a 3 × 3 cut of the DFT sheet, the metal over a Mo or over a hollow (the lower one counts). Silica: the metal in the open corner of a methyl silsesquioxane, Me7Si7O9(OH)3, bound to two siloxide O and one silanol O. On graphene, raw xTB binds H on Ni and Cu about 1.2–1.3 eV too strongly; the offset column adds those per-metal corrections (none fitted for Mn, Mo and W), assuming the error belongs to the metal and not the support. Silica is an insulator: its numbers describe H binding, not an electrode.

    The same sites for O2, CO2 and N2

    The enzymes behind our sites reduce O2, CO2 and N2 too, so every site also gets the intermediates of those reactions: *OH, *O and *OOH; *COOH, *CO and *OCHO; *N2, *N2H, *NH2 and *NH3. From their free energies (computational hydrogen electrode, pH 0) follows the limiting potential: the most positive potential at which every electrochemical step still runs downhill for reductions, the least positive for O2 evolution. All values are GFN2-xTB.

    CO2 and N2 values have no DFT reference yet and carry xTB's full error. A site that binds H more easily than the first CO2 or N2 intermediate will mostly make H2; ΔGH is listed for that comparison (from DFT where computed, else xTB). Zero-point and entropy terms are typical literature values, not computed.

    Will the metal stay in the site?

    A good ΔGH is worth little if the metal atom clusters or dissolves. For every site near the optimum we compute the formation energy Ef = E(metal in site) − E(same vacancy without metal) − E(bulk metal per atom), and from it the dissolution potential Udiss = U°(M/Mn+) − Ef/n. Sites with Ef < 0 and Udiss > 0 V vs SHE pass.

    The dataset

    Everything above comes from one rebuildable database: every metal site with its donors, weak contacts and graphene mapping, the PDB entries and protein entities they come from, motif statistics, the SAC models, DFT results and literature counts, plus the check against the PDB's own metal-link annotation. A public release with a DOI is planned.

    What these numbers are

    • ΔGH = ΔEH + 0.24 eV (computational hydrogen electrode): a thermodynamic first filter, not a rate.
    • PBE-D3 without +U, one spin state per structure, no solvent and no electrode potential.
    • One site per 6 × 6 graphene cell (about 1.9 nm² per metal atom); the protein's second shell is not represented.
    • Literature counts are search hits; a low count is a reason to look closer, not a claim of novelty.
    • All values are drafts from a two-day hackathon and still need expert checking.
    Methods: RCSB PDB Search and Data APIs, gemmi (first shells), GFN2-xTB via tblite (screening), Quantum ESPRESSO 7.1 (PBE-D3, GBRV and pslibrary pseudopotentials), OpenAlex and Europe PMC (literature). Calculations on the Curta cluster, Freie Universität Berlin.
